## Design Context

### Users

The primary user is a private-banking Relationship Manager preparing for a consequential client conversation.
The judged experience focuses on Priscilla Ong preparing to speak with Lau Chi Ming about connected property exposure, collateral risk, and a future redevelopment funding need.
The user must understand the central issue quickly, inspect the supporting evidence, rehearse the client conversation, and leave with an actionable plan.

### Brand Personality

The product should feel calm, credible, discreet, and consequential.
It should communicate private-banking judgment and institutional trust without feeling traditional, ceremonial, or visually heavy.
Urgency should appear only when the data crosses a meaningful threshold.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined editorial private-banking aesthetic with a warm ivory background, charcoal text, restrained burgundy accents, and amber-to-red risk states.
Pair a refined serif for major insight statements with a highly legible sans serif for data and controls.
Use open space, thin structural rules, and an asymmetric information hierarchy instead of repeated cards.
The primary judged experience is desktop-first and opens directly in Lau's case.
It must not resemble a generic analytics dashboard, retail trading terminal, or AI chatbot.
Avoid prompt boxes, assistant avatars, message bubbles, neon dark mode, gradients, glass effects, excessive shadows, and gamified scores.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with one client insight and one prominent **Prepare Lau's meeting** action.
2. Show the LTV threshold before explaining the connected exposure behind it.
3. Use visuals for cause and consequence, with tables serving as supporting evidence.
4. Present specialist agents as a structured decision council with visible disagreement, not a simulated conversation.
5. Make rehearsal choice-based, brief, grounded in client evidence, and focused on reaching a concrete next step.
6. End with an owned, dated action plan and approval-gated connector previews.
7. Distinguish facts, calculations, assumptions, inferences, and proposed actions visually.
8. Preserve Relationship Manager control over every recommendation and external action.

### Primary Interface Sequence

```text
Prepare Lau's meeting
-> reveal LTV cliff
-> explain the same-bet exposure graph
-> calculate collateral and funding consequences
-> show specialist disagreement
-> rehearse two client-conversation decisions
-> approve an owned action plan
-> preview calendar, email, task, and document actions
```

### Signature Elements

- A labeled LTV cliff chart showing current, trigger, and stressed states.
- A same-bet exposure graph linking the client's business, investments, borrowing, and future need.
- A specialist decision table that makes disagreement useful.
- A two-decision rehearsal without chat behavior or numeric personality scoring.
- An Action Bridge that turns the approved outcome into connector previews while preserving explicit human approval.
